Daytrack · editorial review

Empty states and the words around them.

Seventy-six strings reviewed together instead of one screen at a time: every place the app has nothing to show, and every place its vocabulary drifted. The drift is not where I expected it — the writing is strong almost everywhere. What is missing is a named rule, so the weak states have nothing to fail against.

Finding 01

“Nothing” is doing four jobs and only three of them are real

Thirty-six occurrences remain, thirty-three of them in shipping copy, down from fifty-two when this sweep began. Every surviving use was classified, not sampled, and each one now falls in a category the word earns. As a privacy promise it is load-bearing and should never move: Nothing is uploaded, Nothing passes through a server, Nothing is sent anywhere. As a behavioural guarantee it is just as good: Nothing lands until you have seen what it changes. And as a plain statement that no data exists it is simply accurate: a day with nothing written.

Thirteen uses fell in none of those categories and have been rewritten. Four were the ones this finding first named (No step in between, None of it is permanent, No figure here is adjusted or tested, no effect to turn off); two more surfaced on a later pass (the History header and the widget’s nothing left, which collided with the erase-everything headline); six only came out of a full enumeration — the lapse reassurance, the stepper note, the provenance sheet’s means nothing outside this record, the lock hint, and both week-summary mover lines; and the thirteenth arrived with the day-one History screen itself, where Nothing is locked spent the word to deny something the reader had not yet feared. Counting by hand is how the first three passes each missed some.

Finding 02

One condition, two grammars

ONBOARDING
Pick at least one
REPORT BUILDER
Choose at least one slider to put in the report.

Same state — an empty selection. Different verb, length and punctuation. The length difference is defensible (one is a button, one is a note). The verb difference was not. Both settled on pick, and the report note became “Pick at least one slider for the report.”

Finding 03

Four verbs for one idea

The app has already chosen its word, and by a wide margin it is written. That is the right choice — it implies a person and a moment, which is the entire premise of the record.

The competing word was not a one-off. LOGGED was a stat-card heading on the History detail screen, sitting directly beside HIGH and LOW — the most prominent single word in the analysis view. The pair screen’s days logged echoed it. Renaming that card to WRITTEN was the one change that makes the rest self-enforcing: while the header said LOGGED, any future writer would reasonably have assumed that was the house term.

A third word, recorded, survived the first two passes entirely: N recorded days ran through the whole tracker-edit and delete flow, where the stakes are highest. Sweeping one competing verb and declaring the job done is how it hid. The noun the record stays — it names the whole body of days, not the state of one value.

Fourth verb: set, as in the slider’s old no value yet, now not written yet. Keep set for the physical act on the track; never for the state of the data.

Finding 04

The thin-data family has no shared shape

Three messages say “not enough to draw a conclusion,” and one of them is dramatically better than the other two.

PAIRS
Not enough days logged on both to say anything.
no count, no threshold, no way forward
PAIR CHIP
too few days
few compared to what?
REPORT
Only 9 days in this range have anything written. Someone reading it cold cannot tell a pattern from a coincidence at that size… 5 more days and it is worth printing.
count · why · what unlocks it — this is the model

Every thin state should carry those three parts. The pair message became: “9 days have both written. Under 12, a split is as likely to be noise as signal; 3 more and it is worth reading.” The chip became “9 of 12 days”, which needs no adjective at all. Twelve, not fourteen — the pair threshold and the report threshold are different numbers, and reading the code rather than assuming was the difference between an accurate remainder and a plausible one.

Finding 05

The house style already exists — it just has no name

The three best sentences in the app are all empty states, and all three have the same shape: state the fact, then say why it is fine.

“A fresh phone has nothing to disagree with, so there is nothing to choose.”
“They stay blank. The chart will show the hole, and the hole is true.”
“The record starts the first time you write a day.”

Naming that shape is the whole fix. The two weak states fail because nobody told them what to be, not because they were written carelessly.

Finding 06 · found and closed

A range with no written days was announced but never drawn

The string “no days written in this range” existed only as a screen-reader label. A sighted user who lapsed across the whole window got a blank chart with no explanation; a screen-reader user got a sentence. It was the former. The chart is now gated on the same array it draws, and yields to a written message rather than a blank frame: “No days written in this range. The chart draws itself as soon as one is. A gap is kept as a gap. Days are never filled in for you.”

Four rules, so this does not drift again

01
State the fact, then say why it is fine.
Never the fact alone. An empty state that only reports emptiness reads as a failure.
02
Data is written, never logged, entered or recorded.
A day is written or it is blank. A tracker is skipped on purpose. Those are the only three words for the state of a value. The noun is untouched by this: the record is the whole body of days and stays. Two exceptions on the verb, and only two: the printed provenance sheet keeps Patient-entered and Anything unrecorded, which are clinicians’ terms of art and read as evasion if softened.
03
Every “not enough” carries a count, a reason and a remainder.
How many there are, why that is too few, how many more. No bare adjectives — “thin”, “too few”, “not enough” alone are all failures.
04
“Nothing” is reserved for three jobs, and never a fourth.
What leaves the phone, what the app will not do without you, and the plain fact that no data exists. It is the app’s strongest promise; using it as a flourish anywhere else spends credibility the privacy copy needs.
